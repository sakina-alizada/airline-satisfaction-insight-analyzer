# Problem Definition

Airlines collect large volumes of passenger satisfaction survey data after flights. These surveys contain valuable information about passenger experiences, including ratings for services such as seat comfort, inflight entertainment, Wi‑Fi connectivity, check‑in experience, and boarding process. However, analyzing large volumes of survey responses manually is slow and difficult, making it challenging for decision makers to quickly identify the most important drivers of satisfaction and dissatisfaction.

The problem addressed in this project is how to efficiently analyze passenger survey data in order to identify key service factors that influence satisfaction and to predict whether a passenger is likely to report being satisfied or dissatisfied.

This problem is important because passenger satisfaction strongly affects airline competitiveness, brand reputation, and customer loyalty. When airlines can quickly identify which aspects of the travel experience have the greatest impact on satisfaction, they can prioritize improvements that will produce the greatest benefit for passengers.

The useful outcome of this project is an automated analytics pipeline that processes airline survey data, performs exploratory analysis, trains machine learning models to predict passenger satisfaction, and generates a natural‑language insight report summarizing the results. This type of system could help airline managers and customer experience teams better understand passenger feedback and support data‑driven decisions about service improvements.
